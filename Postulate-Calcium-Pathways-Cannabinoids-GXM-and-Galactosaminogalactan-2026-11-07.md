## 1. Cannabinoids CB1/CB2 inhibit Ca2+ influx — [PARTIAL]

### What the record says
- CB1 activation inhibits voltage-gated calcium channels. Anandamide acting at CB1 inhibits high voltage-activated Ca2+ currents in a concentration-dependent manner and reduces Ca2+ influx, inhibiting neurotransmitter release — "a widely accepted general hypothesis" in the Neural Regeneration Research review of anandamide and intracellular Ca2+.
- The canonical CB1 cascade: Gi/o coupling, inhibition of adenylyl cyclase, and Gβγ-mediated inhibition of voltage-gated N-type and P/Q-type Ca2+ channels, with reduced Ca2+ influx and reduced neurotransmitter release.
- In cultured rat hippocampal neurons, CB1 agonists (WIN 55,212-2, anandamide, CP 55,940) inhibited N- and P/Q-type Ca2+ currents; in NG108-15 cells, CB1 activation inhibited N-type voltage-operated Ca2+ channels and decreased Ca2+ influx.
- In oligodendrocytes, the CB1 agonist ACEA inhibited depolarization-evoked Ca2+ transients up to 94%, mimicked by the CB1/CB2 agonist CP55,940 and by anandamide and 2-AG, whereas the CB2-selective agonist JWH133 was ineffective (Glia 2009; PMID 18814177).

### The CB2 counter-evidence — why this is partial, not a clean hold
- In the direct CB1-vs-CB2 comparison (Felder et al., Mol Pharmacol 1995), CB1 mediated inhibition of Q-type calcium channels in AtT-20 cells; **CB2 did not modulate Q-type calcium channels or inwardly rectifying potassium channels under identical assay conditions**, and CB2 did not couple to phospholipase A2, C, or D or to mobilization of intracellular Ca2+ in CHO cells.
- In other cell types CB2 can do the opposite of inhibiting influx: CB2 linked to phospholipase C can produce IP3, trigger Ca2+ release from intracellular stores, followed by capacitative calcium entry. Anandamide can also raise intracellular Ca2+ via TRPV1 in some settings.
- So "CB1/CB2 inhibit Ca2+ influx" is true for CB1 in the tested neuronal settings, and **not true as a blanket statement for CB2** — CB2's effect is cell-type and pathway dependent, sometimes no effect on Ca2+ channels, sometimes a rise in intracellular Ca2+ from stores.

### Verdict 1 — [PARTIAL]
CB1 part: **HOLDS [H→D]**. CB2 part as written: **BREAKS** as a universal claim. The combined sentence as Douglas gave it stays [PARTIAL]. Do not carry "CB2 inhibits Ca2+ influx" forward as a settled fact; carry "CB1 inhibits voltage-gated Ca2+ influx; CB2 is cell-type dependent" instead.

## 2. GXM modulates Ca2+ signaling, blocks Th1

GXM = glucuronoxylomannan, the major polysaccharide of the *Cryptococcus neoformans* capsule (also the *C. neoformans / C. gattii* complex). Split into two claims, because the evidence for them is not equally strong.

### 2a. GXM modulates Ca2+ signaling — [PARTIAL]
- GXM exerts immunosuppression by direct engagement of the inhibitory Fc receptor FcγRIIB. Activation of FcγRIIB by GXM recruits and phosphorylates SHIP, which prevents IκBα activation (J Immunol 2006, 177: 6842–6851).
- The same FcγRIIB / SHIP pathway is described in that literature as carrying immunosuppressive signals that include **inhibition of calcium mobilization**, inhibition of phagocytosis, and inhibition of cellular proliferation.
- That is a documented foothold for "GXM modulates Ca2+ signaling" — via an immune-cell inhibitory receptor, damping calcium mobilization — but the one search did not produce a dedicated study showing GXM directly modulating a Ca2+ channel or a Ca2+ signalling cascade as its primary finding. The wording "modulates" is therefore supported indirectly, not directly.

Verdict 2a: **[PARTIAL] [H→D foothold]** — the FcγRIIB/SHIP → inhibited calcium mobilization link is documented; a direct GXM→Ca2+ mechanism remains [GAP].

### 2b. GXM blocks / dampens Th1 — HOLDS [H→D]
- J Immunol 2006 lists among GXM's effects: "dampen Th1 response," alongside reduced MHC class II expression, inhibited dendritic-cell activation and maturation, reduced T-cell proliferation, and induction of IL-10.
- Nat Rev Microbiol (2016) review: cryptococci "partially repolarize the immune response, at least in mice, from a strong T helper 1 response (TH1 response) towards a weaker TH1 or often a TH2 response, which is less effective at fungal clearance," by blocking dendritic-cell maturation and inhibiting IL-12 and IL-23.
- A medical-microbiology source likewise lists interference with "the development of TH1 immune responses" among the capsule's immunomodulating effects.

Verdict 2b: **HOLDS [H→D]** at the 51% bar. "Blocks Th1" in Douglas's wording is best carried forward as "dampens / repolarizes away from a strong Th1 response" — a dampening, not necessarily a complete block.

### Verdict 2, combined — [PARTIAL], leaning hold
Th1 dampening holds; Ca2+ modulation holds only as a partial, receptor-mediated foothold. Carry both halves with their different strengths, not as one equally-strong claim.

## 3. Biofilm matrix galactosaminogalactan — HOLDS [H→D]

- Galactosaminogalactan (GAG) is the *Aspergillus fumigatus* secreted and cell-wall-associated heteropolymer, composed of galactose and partially deacetylated N-acetylgalactosamine (GalNAc). It "plays a role in a variety of pathogenic processes including biofilm formation, immune modulation and evasion, and resistance to antifungals" (review, PMC9860509).
- As a cationic exopolysaccharide in the extracellular matrix, GAG is an adhesin mediating adhesion and supporting biofilm formation; it cloaks cell-wall β-glucans from innate immune detection and repels cationic antimicrobial peptides.
- "The main ECM polysaccharides include galactomannan and galactosaminogalactan (GAG) that are imperative for biofilm formation" (Curr Opin Microbiol 2024, PMC11391915). Deacetylation by Agd3 renders GAG cationic and biologically active, and is essential for biofilm formation and full virulence; loss of GAG prevents adherent biofilm formation.

Verdict 3: **HOLDS [H→D]**, effectively [D] for the identity and the biofilm-matrix role. Douglas's naming was exact: the biofilm matrix polysaccharide is galactosaminogalactan (GAG).

## Simply put (6th–8th grade parallel telling)
Calcium is a signal inside cells — when calcium rushes in, cells act. Douglas's three ideas, tested:
1. One kind of cannabis signal receiver, CB1, does turn down that calcium rush in nerve cells. The other kind, CB2, does not always do that — sometimes it does nothing to the calcium door, sometimes it lets calcium out of storage inside the cell. So that idea is only partly right as written.
2. The sugar coat of the Cryptococcus fungus, called GXM, does quiet down one arm of the immune system, the Th1 arm. It also seems to turn down calcium signals in immune cells, through a brake receptor called FcγRIIB — that part is real but indirect, not a direct hit on a calcium channel.
3. The sticky mat that Aspergillus fungus builds, called galactosaminogalactan, really is the main glue of its biofilm. That idea holds.
